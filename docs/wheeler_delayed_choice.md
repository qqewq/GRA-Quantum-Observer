# Wheeler's Delayed Choice and Retrocausality

John Wheeler proposed a variant of the double-slit experiment where:

- The decision to observe "which path" is made **after** the particle has already entered the apparatus.
- Experiments have confirmed that:
  - if we choose to observe, we get particle-like behavior,
  - if we choose not to observe, we get wave-like interference,
  - even though the choice is made late.

This inspires talk of **retrocausality**:

> The present choice of measurement seems to determine how the particle behaved in the past.

GRA interpretation:

- The GRA nullifier N is applied at some iteration step:
  - before N: Ψ has many branches, a high Φ,
  - after N: Ψ is collapsed, low Φ.
- In cognitive terms:
  - the agent's **present decision** about how to "look" at the situation
  - selects which historical branch of arguments remains part of "my story",
  - and which branches are forgotten or reclassified as irrelevant.

Thus:

> **N changes the effective cognitive past**, just as Wheeler's experiments suggest that measurement choices can change what we call the "past behavior" of a quantum system.

This is not a claim of physical retrocausality.  
It is a claim that **cognitive retrocausality** is natural once we adopt the GRA viewpoint.
